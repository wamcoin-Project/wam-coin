BITCOINTALK — the announcement thread, a SECOND reply.
======================================================
Posted straight after the payment reply, not days later. The table is
published in this thread, so a reader working on a finding right now is
working against numbers that no longer exist — and a reduction found rather
than announced reads as a reduction hidden, whatever the reasons are.

---------------------------------------------------------------

The bounty table published in this thread was lowered today. The reasons
belong next to it rather than in a changelog nobody opens, so they are here
in full.

  Inflation beyond the 22,000,000 cap, or a way past the treasury rule   3,000 WAM
  A consensus split, or a remote crash of nodes                          2,000 WAM
  Theft of funds held by software in this project                        1,500 WAM
  Credit without entitlement — paying out coins nobody earned            1,000 WAM
  Bulk deanonymisation of miners or users                                  600 WAM
  Denial of service against the pool, the explorer or a seed               400 WAM
  Hardening, a misconfiguration, or a document that misleads               250 WAM

Every amount except the last is lower than it was. The top rung went from
50,000 to 3,000.

Nothing already reported is re-read against it. The 10,250 paid this morning
was judged under the old table and paid at the rungs the reporter set
himself, and every finding already accepted keeps the classification it was
given.

A programme that quietly starts paying less is a programme nobody trusts
twice, so here are the three reasons. They were studied from every side
before anything was touched.

One. The treasury is finite, and we have now measured how fast it empties.
Consensus pays it 2.5 WAM a block until height 400,000 — 750,000 WAM for its
whole life, of which one eighth, 93,750, is this programme's share. 31,500 of
that is already paid, in the programme's first fifty-three days. Under the
old table a single inflation finding, at 50,000, was more than half of
everything the programme had for its entire life. A table that one report can
empty is not a commitment; it is a number waiting to be withdrawn. The new
one can be paid in full seven times over from the 62,250 that is left.

Two. A bounty is not only a cost. It is a distribution of coin that was never
mined, and we have watched what that does. An amount paid under this table
has already been offered to the market in a single block. Nothing in that
broke any rule — a bounty is the finder's coin and his to sell on the day he
likes — but the effect on everyone else is not neutral. 10,250 WAM is seven
hours of the entire network's output arriving in one wallet at one moment.
The question a miner asks before pointing a CPU at a new chain is whether
anything was handed out before he arrived; a lump that size, landing and then
moving, has the same shape as the thing he is asking about, whatever its
reason.

Three. The amounts had drifted away from what this coin costs to make. Miners
take 47.5 WAM a block and the chain makes thirty blocks an hour, so every
machine on this network together earns about 1,425 WAM an hour. That figure
does not move with the hash rate; it is what consensus pays. 10,250 WAM is
seven hours of all of it, or — measured against one person instead of
everyone, on the day it was paid — about 120 days of an average miner in our
pool.

And the direction runs one way only. The hash rate rises, difficulty follows
it, and the block keeps paying the same 47.5 WAM until it halves at height
200,000. So the same sum costs more work to produce every month that passes:
a figure fixed in coin is a figure that grows in labour, and the one above is
a floor rather than a ceiling.

A table has to be priced against something, and ours had stopped being priced
against what the coin is made of.

What did not change. The classes did not: the table still names what it pays
for rather than leaving it to be argued about afterwards. The bottom rung did
not move, because the smallest findings are the ones most often left
unreported and 250 WAM is not what makes or breaks this budget. And the
discretion above the table did not move either — a finding that matters more
than its rung can still be paid more than its rung, and when that happens it
will be said plainly that it was a judgement and not a tier. That matters
more now than it did at 50,000, and it is the reason a lower ceiling is not
a cap.
