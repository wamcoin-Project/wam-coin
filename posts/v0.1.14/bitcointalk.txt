SUBJECT
=======
v0.1.14 — a peer you have already punished cannot buy any more of your node's time

BODY
====
[b]Update your node.[/b]

Verifying one block header costs this chain about twenty milliseconds of RandomX, where Bitcoin spends a microsecond on a double-SHA256. Until this release a peer could buy that again and again for ninety bytes a time.

The header only had to name our tip as its parent, which is public, and carry the difficulty anyone can compute. The node did punish the address — a bad header is Misbehaving, which discourages it — and then accepted its next connection anyway, because an inbound slot was free. Upstream means that leniency and it costs upstream a microsecond. It cost us the whole defence.

Headers from a discouraged peer are now dropped before any proof of work is touched. Held down by a functional test that connects from an address the node will not call local, because a loopback peer is never discouraged and a test written on the loopback would have passed against the unfixed node for the wrong reason.

The same release stops one line from a pool ending the reference miner, brings the Android copy of the miner back in step with the desktop one, corrects a halving figure that was published a block early, and runs six consensus tests that were compiled into every binary and never invoked. What each of those was, and who found it, is in the next post.
